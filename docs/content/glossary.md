# Glossary

## Quantum and quantum-like terms

Decoherence  
loss of phase relations that makes a system behave less quantum-like and more classically mixed

Density matrix  
mathematical object that represents the state of a system, including both uncertainty and coherence

Dissipation  
irreversible loss or redistribution of energy to the environment

Eigenvalue  
number associated with a mode that indicates its strength, rate, or characteristic scale in the model

Eigenvector  
mode pattern associated with an eigenvalue, showing a preferred direction or structure in the system

Hamiltonian  
operator or matrix that specifies the system’s internal energies and couplings and therefore its undisturbed dynamics

Interference  
combination of pathways or amplitudes so that effects can reinforce or cancel one another

Mode  
a characteristic pattern of system behavior, often represented by an eigenvector

Open system  
system interacting with an environment, producing noise and dissipation

Order effect  
outcomes depend on the order of exposures, measurements, or questions

Perturbation  
an external change or intervention applied to test or modify system behavior

Quantum  
physical theory with vector and operator based state descriptions

Quantum biology  
study of whether specifically quantum processes such as coherence, tunneling, or spin effects play functional roles in living systems

Quantum-like  
use of quantum mathematical structure as a modeling language without claiming macroscopic quantum coherence

Spectral  
relating to the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a matrix or operator, used to describe the system in terms of its modes and their strengths

Spectral gap  
difference between selected spectral quantities used as a stability or separation summary

Transition  
change from one regime, state, or dominant mode to another

## Plasticity and learning terms

Attractor  
stable pattern or region toward which system dynamics tend to evolve

Extinction  
reduction of a learned response when the expected outcome no longer occurs, without necessarily erasing the original memory

Fragility  
tendency to undergo large or lasting disruption under perturbation

Generalization  
transfer of a learned response or rule to related contexts or stimuli

Hebbian plasticity  
plasticity rule in which connections are strengthened when units are active together

Homeostatic plasticity  
regulatory adjustment that stabilizes activity when a system becomes too active or too silent

Long term depression  
persistent weakening of synaptic efficacy after appropriate activity patterns

Long term potentiation  
persistent strengthening of synaptic efficacy after appropriate activity patterns

Neuroplasticity  
plasticity in nervous system circuits and dynamics

Plasticity  
capacity to change structure or function with experience

Reinforcement  
process by which an outcome increases the future tendency of a pattern or action

Resilience  
capacity to resist disruption and recover function after perturbation

Synaptic weight  
effective strength of influence from one neuron or node to another

Unlearning  
reduced expression of a prior mapping coupled with new mapping emergence

## Parkinson’s disease related terms

Bradykinesia  
slowness of movement, a core motor sign of Parkinson’s disease

Deep brain stimulation  
therapy using implanted electrodes to modulate abnormal neural activity in specific targets

Digital biomarker  
quantitative feature derived from digital devices that reflects disease state, progression, or response to treatment

Dopamine  
neurotransmitter important for movement, reward, motivation, and learning, especially in basal ganglia circuits

Dopaminergic degeneration  
loss or dysfunction of dopamine producing neurons, especially in the substantia nigra

Freezing of gait  
brief inability or marked difficulty initiating or continuing walking

Gait disturbance  
abnormal walking pattern such as shuffling, reduced arm swing, or freezing episodes

Levodopa  
common medication that increases dopamine availability and often improves motor symptoms

Motor symptom  
movement related sign such as tremor, rigidity, or bradykinesia

Nonmotor symptom  
symptom not primarily about movement, such as sleep, mood, autonomic, or cognitive changes

Parkinson’s disease  
progressive neurological disorder marked by motor and nonmotor symptoms and strongly associated with dopaminergic degeneration

Postural instability  
impaired balance and reflexes that increase the risk of falls

Rigidity  
increased resistance to passive movement, often producing stiffness

Tremor  
rhythmic involuntary oscillatory movement, often most visible at rest in Parkinson’s disease

Wearable sensor  
body worn device used to measure movement, physiology, or daily behavior continuously or intermittently

## Brain and neuroscience terms

Amygdala  
brain structure involved in salience, threat processing, and emotional learning

Basal ganglia  
group of deep brain nuclei involved in action selection, movement regulation, and habit related processing

Biomarker  
measurable feature used to indicate a biological state, process, or disorder

Circuit  
interacting set of neurons or regions performing a function together

Connectivity  
pattern and strength of interactions among neural elements or brain regions

Cortex  
outer layer of the brain involved in perception, cognition, and voluntary control

Edge  
connection between nodes representing coupling, influence, or similarity

Functional connectivity  
statistical dependence between activities of different regions or signals

Graph  
network representation of entities and relations

Hippocampus  
brain structure central to memory formation and contextual representation

Neuron  
excitable cell that processes and transmits information through electrical and chemical signaling

Node  
individual element of a graph such as a brain region, sensor, or model variable

Noise  
random or apparently irregular fluctuations that can obscure or sometimes assist signal processing

Oscillation  
rhythmic variation in neural or system activity over time

Prefrontal cortex  
frontal cortical region strongly involved in planning, regulation, working memory, and behavioral control

Signal  
measurable variation that carries information about the system

Structural connectivity  
physical wiring or anatomical pathways linking regions

Substantia nigra  
midbrain structure important for dopamine signaling and strongly implicated in Parkinson’s disease

Synapse  
junction through which one neuron influences another cell

Synchronization  
alignment of timing or phase across components of a system
