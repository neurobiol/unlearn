# What is Plasticity?

## The short version

The main idea is that plasticity may be better understood as **history-shaped routing** than as simple strengthening or weakening of isolated connections.

A network does not only store **what** happened. It can also be changed by **the order in which things happened**.

## Why this matters

If two systems see the same events but in different orders, they may not end in the same state.

That matters for:

- adaptation,
- repetition suppression,
- rehabilitation,
- context-dependent learning,
- and possibly the transition from compensation to dysfunction in disease.

## A useful picture

Think of the system as moving through a map of possible states.

- One layer says which routes physically exist.
- Another layer says which routes are currently active.
- A third layer says what context or modulation is biasing the motion.

Plasticity then means the map itself slowly changes as the path unfolds.

## What is new in this update

This update pushes beyond a static graph picture in three ways:

1. It treats **structure, function, and modulation** as linked but distinct layers.
2. It treats plasticity as **irreversible and path dependent**.
3. It uses simple history constraints, such as **non-backtracking** or **self-avoidance**, as concrete ways to express adaptation and repetition suppression.

## What does "quantum-like" mean?

Quantum-like does **not** mean “the brain is literally a quantum computer”.

It means we want a compact way to describe situations where:

- context matters,
- order matters,
- and updates do not commute.

In ordinary language: doing A then B may not be the same as doing B then A.

## A summary of our current work

- Weak structure alone does not explain everything;
- Adaptive plasticity matters more than a weak structural floor by itself;
- Model families should be compared by multiple summaries together, not by one recall number alone.

## What are we looking for?

Our framework suggests looking for signatures such as:

- temporal asymmetry in transition statistics,
- hysteresis under repeated stimulation,
- reduction of immediate revisits,
- motif-level changes after experience,
- and changes that depend on experience order rather than just event counts.
