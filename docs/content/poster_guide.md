# How to read the visuals

This poster is designed to be read at two levels: as data and as perception.
The scientific content stands on its own, while the layout deliberately invites reflection on how context can shape interpretation.

## The three panels and what they mean

One panel asks whether individuals with similar movement patterns cluster in feature space, and whether wearable-derived metrics can distinguish Parkinson’s from controls.

One panel presents a time-dependent spectral gap summary. Pronounced peaks suggest transition points in network structure, whereas extended flat regions are consistent with relative stability.

One panel displays a dense similarity network as a visual cue for global structure, connectivity, and potential rewiring across conditions.



## The protocol under the gap plot

- Past exposure and initial learning

- Rest and consolidation

- New learning or unlearning

- Rest

- Reactivation of past learning

This sequence reflects a structured perturbation and recovery design intended to probe how networks reorganize over time.

## Artistic Illusion or Brain Learning

### Did you see a face? 
Some viewers do not see a face at first. After being told there is a face, many suddenly perceive it. This shift illustrates how prior information can reorganize interpretation without altering the underlying stimulus. 

If you focus on the small writing, you might not see the big picture. And if you look from far, then you might not see what is written. This could as well be an analogy of the boundary line(s) between quantum and classical, between physics, chemistry and biology.

And what about neuroscience and psychology? Where do they sit?

### What do you see?
Some viewers report seeing a moon in the sky, a mountain, sharks, or phytoplankton in the ocean. There is no right and wrong answer in art, is there?  

What distinguishes right from wrong, truth from false? Physics? Mathematics? Philosophy? Or Logic? Or all of them? Or none of them? 

The physics of black hole, is it so different from the physics of the atom? Did you wonder why does the poster have a black background? Is it a waste of ink, or just to look nice or different from the norm? Or is it "dark matter"?
